# Ava prompt pack for marketers

Paste one prompt into Ava. Replace every [bracket]. Ava should not invent clients, revenue, or rankings. If a fact is missing, she should say so.

## Offer

1. Write a one-page offer for [business]. The buyer is [who]. The problem is [problem]. The work included is [what you actually do]. End with a single next step. Do not invent results or client names.

2. Rewrite this offer so a person who is not a marketer can understand it: [paste offer]. Keep the price, the work, and the next step. Cut jargon.

3. List the five questions a buyer of [offer] still needs answered before they pay. Phrase each question the way that buyer would ask it.

## Page

4. Outline a landing page for [offer]. Sections: headline, who it is for, the problem, what is included, how delivery works, price, and one next step. No fake testimonials.

5. Write three headlines for [offer]. Each headline names the buyer and the outcome they can check. Do not promise a ranking, a revenue number, or a guaranteed lead count.

6. Write the first screen of a page for [offer] in under 120 words. The last line is the next step.

## Email

7. Draft three short emails for people who asked about [offer]. Email one names the problem. Email two shows the work. Email three asks for the next step. No fake urgency and no countdown.

8. Write a follow-up for someone who went quiet after asking about [offer]. One email. Give them an easy way to say not now.

9. Write a plain receipt email for [product]. Include what they bought, how they get it, and who to reply to if the file does not open.

## Ads

10. Write five primary texts for [offer], each under 90 characters. The reader should know who it is for and what happens next. Do not invent a discount that was not provided.

11. Write three headlines and three descriptions for a search ad about [offer]. Use the words a buyer would type. Do not bid or invent a budget.

12. Turn this page into three social posts: [paste page]. One post teaches, one post shows the work, one post invites a reply. Do not claim a result that is not in the page.

## Leads and follow-up

13. Given this anonymized list description — niche [niche], region [region], company size [size] — write the first sentence of an outreach note that does not pretend you already know the person.

14. Write a qualification checklist for [niche] leads. Mark which fields are required before anyone is contacted. Consent to contact is required. Do not add a step that scrapes a site.

15. Review this follow-up sequence and mark any line that invents urgency, a result, or a relationship: [paste sequence]. Suggest a replacement for each line you mark.
